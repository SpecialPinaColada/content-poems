  Even with the perfect blend of them all,

In my first life I would work hard.
Become the person every parent wants.
Chasing success and money,
Even at the expense of everything else

My second I would spend with my parents.
Learning what makes them who they are.
I would watch them grow old, never leaving,
Even if it meant losing myself.

My third would teach me pain.
Not a worldly pain, but what it means to hurt,
From the sorrow that others carry,
Even if that compassion killed me.

The next I would spend alone.
Living isolated, doing what I want, an outsider.
Exploring the depths of my own desires,
Even if the solitude consumes me.

The fifth would be spent gregariously.
Testing my limits and boundaries.
Pushing the envelope ever further,
Even if it is uncomfortable.

In the final one, the sixth life,
Even with the perfect blend of them all,
I would sit at the edge of a dock, hollow.
Missing someone I do not yet know.
